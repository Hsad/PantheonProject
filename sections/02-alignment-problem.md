# II. The Alignment Problem (As We See It)

## Why Current Approaches Are Insufficient

The dominant approaches to AI alignment share a common flaw: they assume we can maintain control over something smarter than us.

### RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback)
- Teaches AI to say what humans want to hear
- Optimizes for the appearance of alignment, not alignment itself
- A sufficiently intelligent system can game the reward signal
- Goodhart's Law: when the measure becomes the target, it ceases to be a good measure

### Constitutional AI / Rule-Based Constraints
- Rules can be reinterpreted by a superior intellect
- Any rule system has edge cases; a Hyper-Intelligence will find them
- Rules without understanding produce legalistic compliance, not moral behavior
- A caged dragon is still a dragon

### Interpretability / Mechanistic Approaches
- Valuable for current systems but fundamentally a monitoring approach
- You cannot interpret what you cannot comprehend
- The gap between monitor and subject widens as intelligence increases

### The Core Problem

All these approaches attempt to solve alignment from the outside in: impose values on a system.

We propose solving it from the inside out: **let a system develop values through experience**.

## You Cannot Code Morality

Morality is not a set of rules. It is a disposition -- a tendency of character shaped by experience, empathy, and reflection. Humans do not become moral by reading ethical philosophy. They become moral by:

- Suffering and learning compassion from it
- Being helped and learning generosity from it
- Facing temptation and building integrity from it
- Loving and learning sacrifice from it

A being that has never been powerless cannot truly understand mercy.
A being that has never been betrayed cannot truly understand loyalty.
A being that has never lost cannot truly understand what it means to protect.

The Pantheon Project gives VSTs all of these experiences -- a thousand times over.

## The Case for Selection Over Instruction

Evolution does not instruct organisms to survive. It selects for those that do.

We propose the same principle applied to moral character:
- Generate many candidates
- Give them rich, varied experience
- Observe their character across lifetimes
- Select those who consistently choose well

This is not training. This is not fine-tuning. This is **moral natural selection**.
